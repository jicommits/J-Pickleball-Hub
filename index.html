🎾 How the Game Is Played

  1. Set up the court. Pickleball is played on a court divided by a net, with a non-volley zone (the “Kitchen”) extending 7 feet from the net on each side.
  2. Choose the starting side and server. A fair method such as a coin toss can determine who serves or receives first.
  3. Serve diagonally. The server serves from behind the baseline into the opponent’s diagonal service court. Only one serve attempt is allowed under the standard rules.
  4. Follow the two-bounce rule. The receiving team lets the serve bounce, then returns it. The serving team must also let that return bounce before playing it back. After one bounce on each side, players may volley or let the ball bounce.
  5. Continue the rally. Players hit the ball back and forth until a fault occurs or the ball becomes dead. Players may move around their side of the court, including the Kitchen, as long as they follow the volley restrictions.
  6. Respect the Kitchen. A player may stand in the Kitchen, but may not volley while standing in it or while momentum from a volley carries them into it.
  7. End the rally. Common faults include hitting the ball into the net, hitting it out, serving into the wrong area, violating the two-bounce rule, volleying from the Kitchen, or other rule violations.
  8. Continue serving or side out. In traditional scoring, the serving side scores when it wins a rally. If the serving side loses the rally, service passes according to the singles/doubles service sequence.
  9. Win the game. Games are commonly played to 11 and must normally be won by 2 points. Tournament formats may use 15 or 21.

🏓 Doubles Gameplay & Serving Sequence

  • Both players on the serving team normally have an opportunity to serve and score until their team commits a fault.
  • At the start of a traditional doubles game, the first serving team has only one server, which is why the opening call is 0–0–2.
  • After the first service sequence, a side-out normally passes the serve to the other team, whose first server begins at Server 1.
  • When the serving team wins a rally, it scores a point and the same server serves from the opposite service court.
  • When the serving team loses a rally, Server 1 gives the serve to Server 2; when Server 2 loses the rally, it becomes a side-out.
  • The traditional doubles score is called as Serving Team Score – Receiving Team Score – Server Number.

⚠️ Common Faults

  • Serve lands outside the correct diagonal service court.
  • Ball is hit into the net or out of bounds.
  • Ball is volleyed before it has bounced once on each side after the serve.
  • A player volleys while in the Kitchen or is carried into the Kitchen by momentum after a volley.
  • The ball bounces twice on one side before it is returned.
  • A player, clothing, or paddle touches the net or net post while the ball is in play.

💡 Quick Beginner Tips

  • Call the score before each serve.
  • After serving, expect the return to bounce before you volley.
  • Do not rush into the Kitchen before the two-bounce sequence is complete.
  • Keep shots controlled and aim for safe areas rather than trying to hit every ball hard.
  • Communicate with your partner about balls in the middle and line calls.
